# Pattern: Problem-driven Introduction

> Reference pattern, not a mold — adapt, blend, reorder, or depart as the
> material demands. The bar is a section that reads true, not one that
> matches this file.

Best when the problem is widely felt and the gap is easy to state.
Weakness: can feel formulaic if the "pain" is manufactured — the first
paragraph must cite something real.

1. **The problem, concretely** — one paragraph: who hits it, in what
   setting, at what cost. Anchor with a citation or artifact that makes the
   pain undeniable.
2. **Why existing answers fall short** — each prior approach: what it does,
   where it stops. Neutral tone; grounded citations; no strawmen.
3. **The key observation** — the insight that makes the gap closable *now*
   (new data, new method, new regime). This paragraph is the pivot of the
   whole paper.
4. **What we do** — one sentence per contribution, each a checkable claim
   mapped to the section that delivers it.
5. **How the paper reads** — roadmap paragraph (one line per section).

Check before done: paragraph 1 cites something real; paragraph 3 exists
(an Introduction without the key observation is a survey abstract);
every contribution sentence names its delivering section.
